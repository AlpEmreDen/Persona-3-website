Dr. Cosmos – Crew Health Suite
Dr. Cosmos

Crew self-check for microgravity: track today’s vitals and predict long-term bone loss

Three connected tools built on NASA’s Human Research Program hazards and published spaceflight data. Log your daily measurements, review each body system, then see how countermeasures play out over months.

How they connect

  • The daily routine logs vitals over time and links to the self-check for a full body-region review when a reading turns red. A red badge on its tab shows how many measurements are due.
  • In the self-check’s Bones & muscles panel, “Model long-term bone loss” opens the simulator with your exercise level pre-set.
  • The simulator links back to the self-check.
  • Measurements from NASA’s ISS bone and kidney-stone study appear in all three tools: scenario data and validation in the simulator, urine-output and vitamin D checks in the routine, and updated kidney and bone guidance in the self-check.
  • NASA’s bone-loss technical brief and bone-fracture risk record add standards and context (target bone density, vitamin D range, fracture-risk facts) to all three tools.

Sources

All 13 references (NASA Human Research Program, NASA technical briefs, Smith et al. 2015, Zobel et al. 2012 and NASA Technical Reports Server records) are listed on the Sources page, and numbered markers like [2] throughout the tools link there.

Educational research tools, not medical devices or advice. Follow mission medical procedures and your flight surgeon.
